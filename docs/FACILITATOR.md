# Facilitator card · Referral Rescue

**Best transition:** After the deck’s OpenHIE information-flow explanation and at the “Can your table keep Ana’s referral intact?” activity (slide 22 in the 60-minute deck). Use the game as a compact alternative to the card activity, or keep the card activity and share Quick Play afterward. The screen is an educational simulation, not a live exchange.

## 30-second introduction

“Earlier we met Ana, whose information can break at each handoff. Let’s put that journey on screen. You are the referral team. Your job is to keep one trusted story moving from the RHU to the receiving service and back. We will make three choices together. No speed points: tell me why your choice keeps the next team able to act.”

## Live Demo · 2–3 minutes

Open **Live demo**. Read the fictional case card; press **Begin**. The three decisions are relevant context, acknowledgment, and returned outcome. For each, ask for a show of A/B/C fingers, select the room’s choice, then press **Reveal & discuss**. If the room splits, ask one person from each side for a reason. Read the feedback in one sentence and continue.

**Vote prompt 1:** “Which packet helps the receiving doctor act without burying the request in unrelated records?”

**Vote prompt 2:** “A ‘sent’ icon appears. Has the handoff finished? Which signal would you want back?”

**Reveal line:** “The game’s preferred route is [choice]. Notice what changed: the recipient can act, and the origin knows the status. What would this look like in our actual workflow?”

**Closing debrief:** “The referral is not complete just because it left the RHU. Status and outcome need to come back to the team that continues care. Collect once. Protect throughout. Use many times.”

**Two-minute cut:** Show only decisions 1 and 2, stop after the acknowledgment reveal, and say: “The next question is whether the eventual outcome returns to the RHU.” Share Quick Play for the full loop.

**Low-energy room:** “Turn to the person beside you. You have ten seconds to defend A, B, or C. Then we vote.” Do not require a participant device.

## Paper or projection fallback

Read these aloud or print this card. Ask the room to choose A/B/C, then read the answer. There is no need for internet, QR codes, or a projector.

1. **The referral says only ‘Please assess Ana.’** A: Add reason, urgency, relevant summary, requested service, and response route. B: Attach every record. C: Send as is. **Preferred: A.** It is concise and actionable; B exposes unrelated material, C lacks context.
2. **The RHU shows ‘sent.’** A: Obtain receiving acknowledgment and status. B: Treat ‘sent’ as acceptance. C: Copy it into a spreadsheet. **Preferred: A.** The origin needs to know whether the receiver can act.
3. **The receiving facility completes the encounter.** A: Return status, relevant outcome, and follow-up owner. B: Keep the outcome only there. C: Ask Ana to repeat everything at the next visit. **Preferred: A.** The next team can continue care.

**QR/link slide:** Title “Can your team rescue the full journey?”; subtitle “A 3–5 minute fictional case”; use the tested published URL and QR code, plus the human-readable URL below it. Do not use a placeholder QR at the event.

**30-day transfer:** “Which workflow will you improve, who owns it, and what will you measure?” Invite one answer, then let participants write their own. Do not interpret game indicators as an official LHS ML result.
